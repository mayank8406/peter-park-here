# Peter Park Here

For years, Spider-Man had protected Bengaluru. He had stopped criminals, rescued people, and somehow survived the city's roads. But one day, he faced an enemy unlike anything he had seen before. Its roar sounded like **a thousand horns honking at once**. It fired waves of heat, turning Bengaluru's winter into summer. And whenever the rain came, instead of weakening, **it only grew stronger**. This was no ordinary villain. This was the great monster of Bengaluru — **Traffic**.

Spider-Man fought back. He tried to control the chaos, move vehicles, and clear the streets. But the monster kept growing. Then Peter Parker noticed something strange. Cars were parked along both sides of the roads, taking away almost half the usable road. Vehicles had nowhere to go, so they spilled into the remaining space, making the monster even stronger.

Peter finally understood the truth: **Traffic wasn't the real enemy. It was only the monster created by a much bigger problem — parking.**

And that's when Peter Parker became **Peter Park Here**.

## Why We Started

If you've driven around Bengaluru, you have probably experienced it yourself. You reach a busy area because you want to visit a store, meet someone, have dinner, or get something done. You know exactly where you want to go, but you don't know where you're going to park.

At the same time, there may be an unused parking space just a few streets away. A house may have an empty space during the day, an office may have capacity after working hours, or a private property may have a space sitting unused for most of the week.

The demand exists. The space exists. **What is missing is the connection.**

We believe that unused parking can become useful parking if people can discover it when they actually need it.

## How We Solve It

Peter Park Here brings both sides onto one platform.

People with available parking spaces can list them, define when they are available, set the relevant rules, and earn when someone books them. People looking for parking can search around their destination, compare available spaces, understand the price and facilities, check the rules, and reserve before they arrive.

The goal isn't simply to show parking on a map. It is to give people certainty.

Instead of *"I'll reach there and figure out parking,"* the experience should become *"I know where I'm parking."*

## Trust Comes First

A parking marketplace only works when both sides trust it.

Customers need to know that the space they are booking is legitimate, available, and accurately represented. Owners need to know who is using their space, when it is being used, and how their earnings are handled.

That means verification, clear listing information, accurate availability, transparent pricing, secure transactions, and appropriate legal and compliance processes are fundamental to the platform.

## Making Parking Completely Digital

We want to remove the informal friction that still exists around parking.

Discovery, availability, pricing, reservation, payment, and communication should happen through one platform. Owners should be able to manage their spaces and bookings without having to coordinate every transaction manually.

The technology isn't there for the sake of technology. It is what allows a fragmented parking market to work at scale.

## Where AI Comes In

AI can make that experience even simpler.

Instead of searching through multiple filters, a customer could simply say, *"I'm going to this restaurant for two hours. Find me a secure parking space nearby under ₹100."*

The AI assistant can understand the request, search available spaces, check availability, compare options, calculate the price, and help with the reservation.

But the AI doesn't control the business rules. Pricing, availability, authorization, booking, and payments remain controlled by the core platform. AI makes the platform easier to use; it doesn't bypass the systems that make it reliable.

## Starting in Bengaluru, Building for Cities

We're starting with Bengaluru because the problem is impossible to ignore, but the opportunity goes beyond one city.

Growing cities have the same fundamental mismatch: parking demand is concentrated around particular places and times, while available capacity is scattered across homes, offices, shops, apartments, and private properties.

If we can bring that fragmented supply together and build trust around it, one parking space becomes part of a much larger network.

Our ambition is to make parking something people can plan rather than something they have to hunt for.

## Our Mission

Our mission is simple: **to make parking easy to find, safe to use, simple to book, and completely digital.**

We want to take parking from *"Let's drive around and see if we find something"* to *"There's a spot nearby. It's available. It's booked. Let's go."*

**With great traffic comes great parking problems.**
